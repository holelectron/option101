What Unusual Whales Offers

Unusual Whales is one of the best-known retail flow platforms, and one of the broadest toolkits in the category. Its feed covers options trades across U.S. exchanges, with sweeps and multi-leg trades flagged, across stocks, ETFs and indexes. It also includes dark pool data and Greek exposure, including GEX heatmaps and an SPX market maker exposure view. Beyond flow, it is known for its large community, congressional trading data, an AI analyst and a public API.

So an alternative is less about finding more features and more about a different center of gravity: dealer positioning at the heart of the workflow, a live room to learn in, a specific interface, or a narrower tool that does one job.

How to Choose an Alternative

Before comparing names, decide which of these matters most to you.

  • Every print, in context. Where each trade filled (bid or ask side), its premium, its size against open interest, sweep, multi-leg and cross flags, and the next morning's change in open interest.
  • Dealer positioning next to the flow. Whether a print lands on a large GEX node or in an air pocket changes how you read it. Check which exposures a tool shows (GEX, VEX), by strike and by expiration, and how they connect to the feed.
  • Dark pool and company events. Off-exchange prints, earnings dates, insider filings and Congress disclosures explain a lot of unusual flow.
  • Learning and support. Live rooms, lessons and a team you can ask matter as much as the data when you are building a process.
  • A place to practice. Paper trading lets you rehearse a read before you risk money.
  • What each plan actually includes. Headline tiers differ across the category. Compare what you get on the plan you would buy.
⚠️Warning

Options flow describes what traded, not what happens next. An ask-side call print is not bullish on its own: a hedge, a close or one leg of a spread can all print at the ask. Whatever tool you pick, check structure flags, size against open interest and the next morning's open interest before you read direction.

The Best Unusual Whales Alternatives

Skylit (Flowseeker + Heatseeker)

Skylit is built for traders who want flow and dealer positioning in the same place. Flowseeker streams the options tape from full OPRA coverage, every print, with side, premium, Vol/OI and Size > OI, Delta OI the next morning, sweep, multi-leg and cross flags, and a Flow Score (-100 to +100) that ranks how directional a print looks. It is a ranking aid, not a forecast. Around the Live Feed sit the Dark Feed for off-exchange stock prints, Flow Compass (from Market Tide, the whole market's net call and net put premium, down to one ticker's strikes), Company Events with earnings, insider filings and Congress disclosures, and Flow Alerts.

Heatseeker sits in the same web app. GEX and VEX are on one board with a switch between them, rows for strikes and columns for expirations. The board stars the King Node (the node with the highest absolute GEX, positive or negative), and Skylit's framework adds Gatekeepers (prominent nodes between price and the King), Velocity for nodes that are growing or shrinking, Trinity Mode (SPXW, SPY and QQQ side by side by default, and you can change the instruments) and replay. On Pro, Atlas puts GEX, VEX or GEX+VEX levels, flow and dark pool levels on one chart, and Talon, the Skylit AI agent, reads the same live data.

Known for: flow read against dealer positioning, daily Live Stages inside the app, the Trader Success team on every plan, and Nexus (Beta) paper trading.

QuantData

QuantData is one of the more data-dense platforms. It shows real-time options flow across U.S. exchanges, in consolidated views (blocks, splits, sweeps) and trade by trade, with Net Drift and Dark Flow for market-wide and dark pool views. It covers dealer exposure broadly, across strikes and expirations, with a view of how exposure builds through the day. Coverage includes stocks, ETFs and indexes, and its API supports AI agents.

Known for: breadth of metrics in one dashboard.

BullFlow

BullFlow is a real-time options flow and dark pool tracker. Its filters separate sweeps, splits, blocks and multi-leg trades, and a score ranks prints by how unusual they are. It also includes gamma and vanna exposure (GEX and VEX) by strike and expiration, with heatmaps and replay, plus AI-driven alerts. Coverage spans major indexes, ETFs and many stocks.

Known for: an unusualness score and GEX replay.

Cheddar Flow

Cheddar Flow is known for a clean, approachable flow interface. It follows intermarket sweep orders, flags trade conditions such as sweeps and crosses, and includes real-time dark pool data. Its higher-tier plan adds gamma exposure (GEX) across strikes and expirations, with call and put walls, plus AI-driven alerts and an AI assistant for gamma questions.

Known for: an approachable interface.

FlowAlgo

FlowAlgo is known for real-time options order flow built around sweeps and block trades. Its feed labels multi-exchange sweeps and single-exchange splits, and it adds equity block and dark pool order data, with levels drawn from those prints. It also offers AI-driven signals, voice alerts and a trader chatroom.

Known for: sweeps and blocks, voice alerts and a chatroom.

TradeEcho

TradeEcho is an all-in-one platform. It combines options flow (with a sweeps preset), dark pool prints, news, charting and its DealerEdge exposure module in one subscription. DealerEdge shows a GEX heatmap by strike and expiration, with vanna and charm views on the same grid, across indexes, ETFs and stocks, and Cortex is its AI agent layer.

Known for: one subscription for many data types.
